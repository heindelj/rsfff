# hartree pol plan

Card: [[Exchange Polarization Split (Hartree-Product State)]]. Opened 2026-10-31. Status: plan. Builds on rsfff `src/qcgen/almo.py` (fragments → frozen → SCF-MI in FERF spaces → full; orthogonal frozen decomposition; field; C-PCM).

## The idea, and why it is sound here
- One functional domain M: each fragment's occupied orbitals rotate within occ_A ⊕ FERF_A (the nMDQ spaces the SCF-MI already uses; non-orthogonal between fragments). Two energy functionals on M:
  - A(x): the antisymmetrized product (what SCF-MI minimizes; A(x₀) = E_frz, A(x_P) = E_scfmi);
  - H(x) = Σ_A E_A[P_A] + Σ_{A<B} Coulomb(A, B): the Hartree product — each fragment's own functional (its xc included, intrafragment only) plus the full Coulomb coupling of nuclei and densities; no interfragment exchange/xc. H(x₀) − E_ISO = cls_elec exactly (the `_cross_coulomb` we already compute).
- New state x_H = argmin H over M (one freeze-thaw solve); evaluations A(x_H), H(x_P) are free.
- Terms: I_H = H(x_H) − H(x₀) ≤ 0 (pure electrical induction, mutual, with penetration and nonlinearity, in the FERF space); POL = A(x_P) − A(x₀) ≤ 0; **X_POL = POL − I_H** (any sign), resolved as ΔP_onH = P(x_H) − P(x₀) (how antisymmetrizing the electrically polarized orbitals changes the Pauli energy) + R_AS = A(x_P) − A(x_H) ≤ 0 (the further relaxation under the antisymmetrized functional), P ≡ A − H. The endpoint convention I_act = H(x_P) − H(x₀), X_act = POL − I_act is reported alongside (H(x_P) − H(x_H) ≥ 0 is the electrical cost of the exchange-driven deformation).
- The FERF restriction is what makes H-POL meaningful: in the full AO space a Hartree product collapses toward the partner's nuclei (the cage script's XP probe: −84 vs −44 kJ/mol pair, −312 vs −54 oct6). Within occ ⊕ nMDQ the response is the fragment's own dipole/quadrupole/breathing directions, so I_H is the counterfactual "same fragment, no Pauli" that a classical model with the same parents represents. Still check at R < 2.2 Å how much of the Cl⁻ response lands inside the Na⁺ core (density difference on a line, induced dipoles vs the ALMO ones).
- DFT subtlety (the only part of the draft worth keeping): A_T includes interfragment xc, H_T does not, so at ωB97M-V X_POL also carries the change of the interfragment xc beyond exchange along polarization. Quantify it with the existing orthogonal-decomposition dispersion estimator D at x₀, AS(x_H), x_P (`orthogonal_decomposition` + `_disp_part`, no new code): ΔD_POL = D(x_P) − D(x₀). For ions it should be ≪ X_POL; report it, do not re-optimize anything (the draft's §7–9 counterfactual dispersion functionals are not needed for this purpose).
- Relation to existing data: `pol_ref.polarize(..., partners=FD)` is the one-sided, full-AO version (one fragment in frozen partner densities); I_H is its mutual, FERF-restricted version. For Na⁺ partners the two should nearly agree (Na⁺ barely polarizes) — a first check. The complete model: I_H ↔ the dipole/quadrupole/breathing response with the free-ion polarizabilities and the pseudo drive off; X_POL ↔ exclusion drive + confinement stiffening. Fitting those two groups to I_H and X_POL separately instead of both to POL is the payoff.

## Implementation (pyscf, ~1 day)
1. `hartree_pol(system, C0, bases, atom_slices, D0, tol, max_cycle)` in `src/qcgen/almo.py`: per fragment F_A = h_A + J[P_A] + Vxc_A[P_A] + Σ_{B≠A}(−V_nuc,B + J[P_B]) (h_A = `_fragment_hcore`, the fragment's veff = `frag_energy_fock` of `orthogonal_decomposition`, shared supermolecule grids); project F_A into bases[A] (orthonormal within the fragment), diagonalize, keep n_occ; freeze-thaw all fragments with DIIS on the stacked projected Fock/density commutators; converge to |[F,P]| < 1e-6. Energy H(x) assembled as Σ_A E_A[P_A] + `_cross_coulomb` (reuse). Cost ≈ one SCF-MI.
2. `almo_eda(..., hartree=True)`: after the SCF-MI compute x_H, A(x_H) (`sup.energy(density(C_H, S))`), H(x_P), H(x₀), H(x_H); add terms `pol_elec` (I_H), `pol_exch` (X_POL), `dP_onH`, `R_AS`, `pol_elec_act`, `pol_exch_act`, optional `disp_pol` (ΔD_POL); per-fragment induced dipoles for both states in `states`.
3. Checks: closure POL = I_H + X_POL to 1e-10; I_H ≤ 0, R_AS ≤ 0; large R: X_POL → 0 and I_H → −½ Σ α_A E_A² (free-ion α from `pol_ref.field_response`); gradient of H by finite rotations; isolated fragment: everything 0; with `pol_model="almo"` the AO-space collapse reproduced as a warning case.
4. Scan script `scripts/almo_xpol_scan.py`: Na–Cl 1.9–8 Å, Cl–Cl 2.8–8 Å, Na–Na, plus the nine cage shapes at 2.6/2.8/3.2 Å (`ion_cage_response.shell`); plot I_H, POL, X_POL (and its two pieces), the endpoint convention, ΔD_POL, and the complete model's e_pol with the pseudo drive off / on against I_H / POL.
5. Later: C-PCM (the product density is the sum, G[Σρ_A] applies as is), the FF labels (`pol_elec`/`pol_exch` columns in the EDA jsonl), and a Q-Chem-free route for molecular fragments once the FERF truncation question is settled.
